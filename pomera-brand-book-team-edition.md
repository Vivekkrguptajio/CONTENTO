# PART 1 — THE COMPANY

## 1.1 What Pomera is

Pomera is an **alternative ad platform** for D2C brands in India.

A brand commits a budget at a fixed cost per thousand verified views. Their video content is distributed as short vertical posts across a network of independent publishers and posted from their own social accounts. Every view is verified, fraudulent traffic is filtered out, and publishers are paid per verified view via UPI. The brand is billed only for views that were actually delivered and verified.

## 1.2 The core idea in one paragraph

A D2C brand spending on Meta commits money without knowing what it will get. Impressions are auction-priced, costs move without warning, and the relationship between spend and outcome only becomes clear after the money is gone. Pomera sells the opposite: a fixed price, a verified unit, and payment only on delivery. The brand knows its number before it spends.

## 1.3 The problem, both sides

**Demand side.** The frustration of a D2C founder buying paid social is not the price. It is the uncertainty. They cannot forecast what a rupee buys, cannot explain outcomes to investors or partners, and cannot repeat a good month reliably.

**Supply side.** A large population of skilled video editors in India can produce content that performs but has no route to being paid for it. Influencer and creator platforms gate access by follower count, which excludes nearly all of them. Their skill has no market.

Pomera connects the two: uncertainty on one side, unused capacity on the other.

## 1.4 How it works

1. Brand commits a budget and a fixed rate per 1,000 verified views
2. Brand provides video content — existing long-form material, raw footage, or product video — plus guidelines
3. Independent publishers produce short vertical videos and publish them from their own accounts
4. Every post is tracked; views are verified and fraudulent traffic filtered
5. Publishers are paid per verified view, weekly, via UPI
6. Brand receives a performance report and is billed only for verified delivery

## 1.5 Why it is structurally different from paid social

- Distribution runs through real accounts with real audiences rather than paid placement, which changes how platform algorithms treat the content
- Pricing is fixed and outcome-linked rather than auction-based
- India-native by design: ₹-denominated, UPI payouts, WhatsApp-first operations, low minimum spend

## 1.6 Aim

To become the advertising channel Indian D2C brands use when they need certainty about outcome — a permanent line in the media budget, not a service experiment.

## 1.7 Objectives

**0–3 months**
- Establish real delivery data: the actual achieved cost per thousand verified views
- Build a network of 100 active, reliable publishers
- Complete three pilot campaigns and convert the first paying brands
- Reach 30 paying brands

**3–12 months**
- Repeatable brand acquisition with a predictable cost per acquired customer
- Brand repeat rate above 50% within 30 days of first campaign — the true measure of whether this works
- Automated view verification replacing manual checking
- Software platform replacing manual operations
- Sales attribution: coupon codes and tracked links proving clicks and code use (roadmap, section 9.2)

**12 months+**
- Purchase data matched back to posts, giving cost per purchase (roadmap, section 9.2)
- Pricing on outcomes further down the funnel than views
- Category expansion beyond D2C
- A standing budget line alongside existing ad platforms

## 1.8 Scope

**In scope:** two-sided marketplace; campaign creation, briefs and content delivery; view tracking, verification and fraud filtering; collection from brands and payouts to publishers; performance reporting; management of the publisher network; Indian market, ₹, UPI.

**Out of scope for now:** producing content for brands (we distribute, we don't produce); enterprise advertisers; international markets; coupon codes, tracked links and purchase data (on the roadmap, built only after view delivery is proven); any full software platform before manual operations demonstrably break.

# PART 2 — NAME AND VERBAL IDENTITY

## 2.1 The locked set

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Pomera |
| **Descriptor** | alternative ad platform |
| **Brand-facing tagline** | Know your number before you spend. |
| **Publisher-facing tagline** | Paid per view, not per follower. |

These four are final. They do not get rewritten per campaign, per designer, or per channel.

## 2.2 Name usage rules

- Written **Pomera** in body copy, legal documents, contracts, invoices and prose. Always capitalised P, never all-caps, never POMERA.
- Never abbreviated. Not "Pom," not "PMR."
- Never possessive in the product sense — it's "the Pomera network," not "Pomera's network," where possible.
- The name carries no meaning in any language. This is deliberate. It does not need explaining, and nobody should invent a backstory for it.
- The company is referred to as a singular entity: "Pomera is," not "Pomera are."

## 2.3 The four layers and what each one does

Understanding these prevents the most common branding mistake — trying to make one line do four jobs.

| Layer | Job | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Name** | Identifies | Everywhere |
| **Descriptor** | Says what category we're in | Beside the name, bios, deck cover, legal |
| **Tagline** | Says why the reader should care | Hero sections, ads, campaign headers |
| **Positioning statement** | The full internal argument | Internal only, never shown publicly |

## 2.4 Positioning statement (internal, never published)

> For founder-led D2C brands in India who are spending on paid social without certainty about what it buys, Pomera is an alternative ad platform that distributes their video content through a network of independent publishers and charges a fixed rate per verified view. Unlike auction-based ad platforms, Pomera fixes the price before the spend and bills only for delivered, verified views.

## 2.5 Why two taglines

Brands and publishers want opposite things. Brands want certainty about spend. Publishers want income without gatekeeping. A single line serving both serves neither.

The two lines almost never appear together in public, because the audiences almost never share a surface. A brand founder does not read publisher recruitment messages. A publisher does not read the brand pitch deck. They appear together only in internal documents and the pitch deck, where showing both demonstrates that we understand a two-sided market.

Pomera is **one company and one brand**. The publisher side has its own front door, **ClipperCircle by Pomera**, because publishers respond to a name that tells them instantly the space is for them, and because "circle" says community, not just a job. It is an **endorsed sub-brand**, not a second brand.

| | Pomera | ClipperCircle by Pomera |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | Brands, agencies, investors | Publishers |
| Website | pomera.com | clippercircle.pomera.com (clippercircle.com redirects here) |
| Dashboard | Brand dashboard, header "Pomera" | Publisher dashboard, header "ClipperCircle by Pomera" |
| Instagram | @pomera — display name "Pomera \| Alternative Ad Platform" | @clippercircle — display name "ClipperCircle by Pomera \| Paid per view" |

| What we call the supply side | Publishers, the publisher network | Clippers (and publishers in agreements and payouts) |
| Logo | Beam + pomera wordmark | Same Beam + "ClipperCircle" + small "by pomera" |

**How it works behind the scenes:** one system. A brand launches a campaign in Pomera; it appears in the ClipperCircle dashboard; publishers post; views are verified; the brand is billed and publishers are paid.

**Rules**
1. **"by Pomera" always appears** with the name on every ClipperCircle surface: Instagram display name, website, dashboard header, WhatsApp group name, emails. That line is what keeps it one brand.
2. **Spelling:** *ClipperCircle* — one word, capital C twice. Never "Clipper Circle", "Clippercircle" or "CC". Full form *ClipperCircle by Pomera* on first mention; *ClipperCircle* alone is fine after that, on publisher surfaces only.
3. **ClipperCircle never has its own visual identity.** Same Beam, Geist, ink, paper and lime rules as Pomera. No separate logo, colour or font.
4. **On brand-facing surfaces, ClipperCircle is mentioned only when it's useful, in one line:** *"Publishers join through ClipperCircle, our publisher community."* It is never hidden and never the headline.
5. **The category doesn't change.** ClipperCircle is the community of people who distribute Pomera campaigns. Pomera is still an advertising platform, never a clipping platform (section 3.1).

# PART 3 — POSITIONING

## 3.1 The category we claim

**Advertising.** Not editing, not clipping, not content production, not influencer marketing.

This is the single most important strategic decision in the company, and it was made for a specific reason: language describing the *labour* places us in a small budget line and makes us look replaceable by hiring an editor. Language describing the *advertising outcome* places us alongside the platforms brands already fund with crores.

## 3.2 Hook, then mechanism

The descriptor creates curiosity. The mechanism creates belief. **Never use the hook alone** — unsupported, it sounds like every ad vendor's marketing.

- **Hook:** "Pomera is your alternative ad platform."
- **Mechanism, the moment anyone asks "alternative how?":** "Meta charges you for impressions with no certainty about outcome. Pomera delivers verified views at a fixed ₹CPM through a network of real accounts. You know your number before you spend, and you pay only for what was delivered."

## 3.3 We do not position against Meta as a replacement

We are **the portion of a media budget that buys certainty** — typically 10–20%. This framing matters commercially: it lets a brand adopt Pomera without anyone internally having to concede that their existing strategy was wrong. "Replace your ads" makes an enemy of the person who chose those ads. "Add certainty to part of your budget" makes them an ally.

## 3.4 What we compete against, honestly

| Competitor | Their advantage | Our answer |
|---|---|---|
| Meta / Google ads | Scale, targeting, familiarity | Certainty of price and delivery; different budget slot, not a replacement |
| Influencer marketing | Recognisable faces | No follower gatekeeping means far more posts, far lower cost, performance-linked pricing |
| Hiring an editor | Full control | An editor makes content; we deliver distribution and verified reach |
| International content-reward platforms | Software maturity, scale, proof | India-native: ₹ pricing, UPI payouts, WhatsApp operations, low minimum, managed campaigns, local brand knowledge |

## 3.5 The known competitive risks

**Fee comparison.** The leading international platform in this model charges a flat 10% platform fee, stated openly. Pomera's margin is embedded inside an all-in quoted rate. These are not equivalent — theirs is self-serve software charging on top of a rate the brand sets; ours is a managed service where we source publishers, write briefs, run campaigns and handle payouts. But if a brand benchmarks the two, "10%" is the number in their head, and the team must be able to answer it calmly with the managed-service distinction.

**Verification depth.** Mature platforms in this space run multi-layer fraud detection, direct platform API connections and independent third-party verification. At launch, Pomera verifies through tracked links, submitted analytics and manual spot-checking. That is honest for a founding phase. **Never imply capability we don't have.** Describe exactly what we do.

# PART 4 — AUDIENCES

## 4.1 Brands (demand)

**Who:** founder-led D2C brands in India.

**Qualification criteria — all four:**
1. Already spending ₹50,000+/month on paid social (proves budget and proves pain)
2. Has existing video assets or long-form content
3. Sells online with a functioning checkout
4. Category appropriate for publication on individual publishers' personal accounts

**Explicitly out of scope: enterprise advertisers.** Sales cycles run months, procurement and brand-safety gates are prohibitive pre-launch, and dependence on one large client is a structural risk. Large advertisers appear in the deck to illustrate market size — never as sales targets.

**What they actually feel:** not excitement. Anxiety. They have spent lakhs and cannot fully explain what it bought. **The emotion we sell is relief from uncertainty, not excitement.** This is why our brand-facing tone is calm and plain rather than loud — hype is what every ad vendor already sounds like, and hype is the opposite of accountability.

## 4.2 Publishers (supply)

**Who:** independent video editors, typically 500–20,000 followers. Students, freelancers, second-income seekers, hobbyist editors. On Pomera they are called **publishers**: they do more than edit, they publish from their own accounts, and that is the part brands pay for. Inside **ClipperCircle by Pomera** we speak to them as **clippers**, the word they use for themselves (section 8.5).

**Where they meet us:** clippercircle.pomera.com, @clippercircle on Instagram, the ClipperCircle WhatsApp group, and the publisher dashboard.

**No minimum follower requirement, ever.** This is a defining principle, not a temporary policy. It is the reason this network can exist at scale when influencer platforms cannot, and it is the single line that converts this audience.

**What they actually feel:** exclusion. They have been rejected by influencer platforms for not having enough followers, and their skill has no market. **The emotion we sell is access plus income** — you're in, and you get paid.

# PART 5 — MESSAGING ARCHITECTURE

## 5.1 Brand-side message hierarchy

**Level 1 — The promise**
> Know your number before you spend.

**Level 2 — The three supporting pillars**

| Pillar | Claim | Evidence to cite |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed price** | You set the rate per 1,000 verified views before the campaign runs. No auction, no drift. | Campaign terms, rate card |
| **Verified delivery** | You pay only for views that were delivered and verified. Fraudulent traffic is filtered. | Verification process, campaign report |
| **Real distribution** | Content is published from real accounts with real audiences, not paid placement. | Post links, account list |

**Level 3 — Proof.** Campaign reports with delivered views, achieved CPM, post links. *(Currently none exists. See Part 6.)*

**Message order in any brand conversation:** problem → promise → mechanism → proof → ask. Never lead with the mechanism; a brand does not care how it works until they care about the outcome.

## 5.2 Publisher-side message hierarchy

**Level 1 — The promise**
> Paid per view, not per follower.

**Level 2 — The three supporting pillars**

| Pillar | Claim |
|---|---|
| **No gatekeeping** | No follower minimum. Your skill decides your earnings, not your audience size. |
| **Your account, your content** | You post from your own account. You keep it, and you keep your audience. |
| **Real, regular money** | Paid per verified view, weekly, via UPI. |

**Level 3 — Proof.** Payout records and publisher earnings. *(Currently none exists.)*

**Where these messages live:** on ClipperCircle surfaces. Publisher copy never appears on @pomera or the brand website beyond the routing link.

**Message order in any publisher conversation:** offer → objection-killer → mechanism → proof → ask. The objection-killer ("no follower minimum") must come early, because this audience's first assumption is that they'll be rejected again.

## 5.3 Objection handling

**Brand objections**

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "How is this different from influencer marketing?" | Influencer pricing is per person and fixed regardless of performance. Ours is per verified view. No follower gatekeeping means many more posts at a far lower cost per view. |
| "How do I know the views are real?" | Every post is tracked and verified, and you are billed only on verified views. Describe exactly the method we currently use — never more. |
| "What if the content flops?" | You pay per delivered view. A post that does not perform does not generate a bill. Your risk is capped by your budget. |
| "Can I control what they post?" | Yes. You set the brief and guidelines, and content is reviewed against them. |
| "Why not just hire an editor?" | An editor produces content. That still leaves you needing distribution. We deliver reach across many real accounts. |
| "Another platform charges 10%." | That is self-serve software. We are a managed service — we source the publishers, write the briefs, run the campaign and handle payouts in ₹ via UPI. Our rate is all-in. |
| "Are you affiliated with Instagram or Meta?" | No. Never imply otherwise, in any material. |
| "What is ClipperCircle?" | Our publisher community — the independent video editors who publish Pomera campaigns from their own accounts. Same company, same verification, same billing. |
| "Will my video go viral?" | We can't promise that, and nobody honestly can. Posts from real accounts feel like content, and many posts give your campaign many chances. You pay the same fixed rate per verified view either way. |

**Publisher objections**

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Is this real?" | Show the process and, once they exist, real payout records. Before that, be direct about being early and what founding members get. |
| "I don't have enough followers." | There is no follower minimum. Ever. |
| "When do I get paid?" | Weekly, via UPI, on verified views. |
| "What if my clip doesn't perform?" | You earn on the views you get. State this plainly — never promise virality. |
| "Do I have to give up my account?" | No. It's your account, your content, your audience. |
| "What's the difference between ClipperCircle and Pomera?" | Same company. Pomera is the ad platform brands use. ClipperCircle by Pomera is where clippers join, pick campaigns and get paid. |

## 5.4 Investor deck

The investor deck (*Pomera Investor Deck*, Claude Slides, 12 slides) is the approved way to present Pomera to investors. Its order is the argument: cover → brand problem → unused publisher capacity → how it works → market → business model → funnel roadmap → competition → risks → where we are → team and ask → thank you.

Rules for any version of it:
- **We are not raising yet.** The ask is introductions to founder-led D2C brands for three free pilots, honest feedback, and introductions to people who have built marketplaces or ad verification. Change the ask only with founder sign-off.
- **Every market figure carries its source** on the slide. Only figures from section 6.3 are used.
- **Risks are shown, not hidden.** The risks slide stays in every version: verification depth, disclosure rules, the 10% comparison and our unproven CPM.
- **Roadmap stages are labelled as roadmap.** Nothing on the roadmap is ever shown as capability we have.
- **Two-sided slide:** show Pomera (brands) and ClipperCircle by Pomera (publishers) as one company with two front doors.
- **Early stage is stated plainly:** no revenue, no pilots run, no money raised, until that changes.
- **Look:** ink and paper, Geist, lime only as a signal on dark slides, one full-lime slide at most (the thank-you slide).

# PART 6 — PROOF RULES

## 6.1 The absolute rule

**No fabricated numbers. This overrides every other consideration, including a better-looking page, deck or post.**

Pomera has **zero delivery data.** No verified view has been produced by this company.

**Permanently prohibited in every asset:**
- The inherited figures from the previous venture: 1,200+ network size, ₹4.2L paid out, 8.6M views, 34 campaigns, ₹12 average CPM
- "Trusted by" logo strips for brands we do not work with
- Campaign mockups using real people's or real brands' names without permission
- Any invented case study, testimonial or earnings figure
- "India's first" or comparable unverifiable superlatives

**Why this is non-negotiable:** our entire product promise is *verification*. A company that inflates its own numbers cannot credibly sell verified views. Fabricated client logos are legal exposure. And a single invented figure, once checked, permanently ends an investor relationship.

## 6.2 What to say before proof exists

Honest founding-stage language does the work that social proof will do later:
- "Founding cohort now open"
- "First campaigns free — we run it, you see the report, then decide"
- "Per-view payouts, weekly via UPI"
- Founder story and the mechanism explained clearly

Any projection must be labelled a projection.

## 6.3 Market benchmarks the team may cite

These are external figures from the market, not Pomera performance, and must always be described that way.

| Figure | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Established platform creator payouts | roughly ₹42–₹170 per 1,000 views | International market rates |
| Meta CPM in India | roughly ₹150–₹400 per 1,000 impressions | Varies widely by category |

**Sourced market figures for the deck and outreach.** Cite the source every time.

| Figure | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Digital advertising in India, 2025 | ₹94,700 crore, up 26%; about 63% of all ad revenue | FICCI-EY Media & Entertainment report, 2026 |
| Digital ad spend from SMEs and long-tail advertisers | ₹36,300 crore, from over a million advertisers | FICCI-EY Media & Entertainment report, 2026 |
| Influencer marketing in India | ₹3,375 crore by 2026 (projection) | EY and Big Bang Social, 2024 |
| Creators who monetise effectively | 8–10% of Indian creators; about 2–2.5 million people | BCG, *From Content to Commerce*, 2025 |

**Retired:** "100M+ creators in India". It is not stated in the BCG report and must not be used. We do not claim a total addressable market until achieved CPM exists.

**Important correction to legacy material:** the old claim of being "10–30x cheaper than paid ads" is not supportable. Based on real market rates, the realistic saving is roughly **3–5x**. Use that, and only once our own data exists.

## 6.4 The one number that matters

**The real, achieved cost per thousand verified views.**

Until it exists, every claim rests on an assumption. Judge every activity against one question: *does this get us closer to a verified view?*

# PART 7 — PROMISES

## 7.1 What we can promise

- A **fixed price** per 1,000 verified views, agreed before the campaign runs
- Billing **only on verified delivery**
- **Transparent reporting** — post links, view counts, achieved CPM
- **Weekly UPI payouts** to publishers, on time, every time
- **A make-good** if we under-deliver: the campaign extends, or the shortfall is not billed
- **No follower minimum** for publishers

## 7.2 What we can never promise

- Guaranteed virality
- Guaranteed view volume *(unless we have chosen to carry that risk explicitly with a make-good clause in writing)*
- Guaranteed sales or conversions
- A specific CPM, before our own delivery data exists
- Anything implying affiliation with Instagram, Meta, YouTube or any platform

**The distinction to hold in mind:** we guarantee **price and risk**, not **outcome**. Virality is not controllable, and a promise we cannot keep destroys the one thing we are selling.

## 7.3 Payout integrity

Publishers are paid on time, every time, even when a campaign under-performs. Reputation on the supply side is the business. A missed payout costs more than any single campaign is worth.

## 7.4 How we talk about virality

People skip ads. Posts from real accounts feel like content, so people are more willing to watch them. That is a real reason Pomera works, and we may say it. What we may not do is turn it into a promise.

| Say | Never say |
|---|---|
| Posts from real accounts feel like content, not ads. | We make your brand go viral. |
| Many posts across many accounts give each campaign more chances to travel. | Guaranteed viral reach. |
| You pay the same fixed rate per verified view, whether a post takes off or not. | Your video will blow up. |
| Some posts will outperform. We report every one, good or bad. | Viral content on demand. |

To publishers (clippers, on ClipperCircle surfaces): "You earn on every verified view your post gets. If it travels, you earn more." Never "go viral and earn lakhs".

The reason is commercial as much as ethical. We guarantee **price and billing**, never outcome. The first time a promised viral campaign doesn't take off, we lose the one thing we sell, which is certainty.

## 7.5 Disclosure on every paid post

Every Pomera campaign post is labelled. Publishers are paid to post, which ASCI treats as a material connection. The label depends on the **campaign type**, which every brief states:

| Campaign type | When | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand campaign** | The post promotes a product, service or brand, or carries a code or link. Every D2C campaign. | Instagram's "Paid partnership" label, or "Ad" / "#ad" at the start of the caption. Required. |
| **Content distribution** | The post spreads someone's own content (podcast, show, founder talks) with no product pitch, code or link to buy. | "Collab" at the start of the caption, or the owner added as a collaborator. Cautious default; confirm with a lawyer before the first campaign of this type. |

If in doubt, it is a brand campaign. A content-distribution clip that keeps a sponsor read or product plug becomes a brand campaign and needs the paid label.
- The label requirement is written into every brief and every publisher agreement.
- A post without the label is not counted and not paid until it is fixed.
- Nothing in a post, brief or report may suggest that Pomera is affiliated with Meta, Instagram, YouTube or any platform.

# PART 8 — VOICE

## 8.1 Principles

1. **Numbers and money first, adjectives last.** "Earn ₹3,000–8,000 a month editing on weekends" beats "unlock your creative potential."
2. **Short sentences. Active voice. Second person.**
3. **Plain over clever.** We sell accountability. Clever copy reads as marketing; plain copy reads as true.
4. **Specific over general.** "We paid ₹3,800 to a publisher with 900 followers for one clip" stops the scroll. "Great earnings potential" does not.
5. **Calm, not loud.** Especially brand-side. Our buyer is anxious, not excited.
6. **CTAs state what happens.** "Launch your first campaign," "Start earning," "Join the founding cohort." Never "Submit," never "Learn more."

## 8.2 Tone by audience

| | Brands (Pomera) | Publishers (ClipperCircle by Pomera) |
|---|---|---|
| Register | Precise, accountable, confident | Direct, money-first, energetic |
| Language | English | English; Hinglish acceptable on Instagram and WhatsApp |
| Sentence length | Short to medium | Short |
| Proof shown | CPM, view counts, reports | Payout records, earnings |
| What we never do | Hype, urgency tricks, false scarcity | Over-promise earnings, imply guaranteed virality |

## 8.3 Words we use

**Pomera (brand-facing):** campaign · verified views · fixed rate · ₹CPM · delivery · reach · distribution · publishers · publisher network · payout · brief

**ClipperCircle (publisher-facing):** campaign · clippers · ClipperCircle · verified views · payout · UPI · brief · founding cohort

## 8.4 Words we never use

**Everywhere:** clip farm · distribution service · influencer marketing · UGC agency · India's first · guaranteed views · revolutionary · game-changing · disruptive · seamless · effortless · unlock · supercharge · go viral · viral guaranteed

**On Pomera brand-facing surfaces, additionally:** clipping platform · clippers · clipping

| clippers (ClipperCircle surfaces only) | editors (as a role), creators |

# PART 9 — BUSINESS MODEL

## 9.1 Pricing and margin

- The brand is quoted a **single all-in rate** per 1,000 verified views. The split beneath it is not published.
- Pomera's margin is built into that rate and covers verification, fraud filtering, campaign operations, network management, payouts and customer service. The exact split is confidential to the founders.
- **Minimum campaign: ₹10,000.**
- If a brand asks about margin directly, the honest answer is that margin is built into the rate. This is standard practice for advertising networks and marketplaces. **We never misrepresent it** — we simply don't publish the split.
## 9.2 The funnel roadmap

Today Pomera sells the top of the funnel. The roadmap completes the funnel, so brands can see what their spend did all the way to a purchase.

| Stage | When | What it adds | What the brand sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Top of funnel** | Today | Verified views at a fixed ₹CPM | Views, post links, achieved CPM |
| **Middle of funnel** | Roadmap | A coupon code and tracked link (UTM) for each publisher and post | Clicks and code use, by publisher and by post |
| **Bottom of funnel** | Roadmap | Purchase data from the brand's store, matched back to each post | Purchases and cost per purchase, by publisher and by post |

Rules:
- **Each stage is built only after the one before is proven** with real data. Tracked links and codes come first; purchase matching follows once they work reliably.
- **Roadmap stages are always labelled as roadmap** in every deck, page and conversation until they exist. Never sell them as current capability.
- **Pricing stays per verified view** until outcome data is proven. Any move to pricing further down the funnel is a separate founder decision.
- Purchase data belongs to the brand. We use it only to report on that brand's campaigns, with the brand's written consent, handled under India's data protection law (DPDP Act).

How to say it in one line: *"Today we sell verified views. Next, we follow them to the purchase."*
